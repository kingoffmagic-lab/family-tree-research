# Finding a historical locality or volost

Start with the locality as written, event date, record type and religious community when documented. Preserve alternatives; identical village names do not establish identity.

1. Find a dated settlement list, gazetteer or map using the [historical routes](../data/jurisdictions.json). Distinguish the statistical reference year from publication year.
2. Reconstruct the dated hierarchy: state, gubernia/oblast, uyezd/district, volost and settlement. Search spelling and transliteration variants separately.
3. Determine the parish, synagogue, mosque or other record-creating institution independently of civil boundaries. The nearest building is not proof of membership.
4. Consult the country and region directory, then the institution's fonds and inventories. Record the evidence connecting the record creator to its custodian.
5. Check transfers, predecessor archives and external custodians. Return multiple candidates when the place or keeper is unresolved.

Examples: a Livonian locality may require both Latvian and Estonian catalogues; a document using an older city name requires dated identification before selecting a present region. Finnish imperial-period records can remain in Finland. These are research scenarios, not assertions that a particular family record exists.

A volost need not have a separate archive. Local catalogue keyword results are discovery routes, not an automated custody map. Save exact document references and family queries only in the private case.
